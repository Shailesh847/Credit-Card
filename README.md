Credit Card Analytics Dashboard
Project Overview

This project started with a simple question: What can customer and transaction data tell us about credit card business performance?

To answer this question, I worked with SQL and Power BI to build an interactive Credit Card Analytics Dashboard. The main purpose of this project was not only to create attractive visuals, but to understand how data can be imported, updated, analyzed, and converted into meaningful business insights.

I started by importing the credit card data from SQL into Power BI. After connecting the data, I explored the available fields and started looking at customer behavior, transaction performance, revenue, interest, card categories, and other business metrics.

As I continued working, I created two different dashboards:

Credit Card Transaction Report
Credit Card Customer Report

The project became more interesting when I went back to SQL and added and updated data in the database. I then refreshed the data in Power BI to see how the dashboard responded to the changes.

This helped me understand something important: a dashboard is not a static report. When the source data changes, the analysis can also change.

Key Business Insights

After analyzing the dashboards, I found several interesting patterns.

Overall Performance
Total revenue was approximately 57M.
Total interest was approximately 8M.
Total transaction count was approximately 667.2K.
Total transaction amount was approximately 45.5M.
Customer Insights

Male customers contributed approximately 31M in revenue, while female customers contributed approximately 26M.

Businessmen were the highest revenue-contributing customer job group, followed by White-collar and Self-employed customers.

Graduate customers contributed the highest revenue among the education groups.

Card Category Insights

Blue and Silver cards were the strongest-performing card categories and together contributed around 93% of overall transactions.

This shows that a large portion of transaction activity was concentrated in these two card categories.

Geographic Insights

The states of Texas, New York, and California were major contributors to revenue.

Together, these three states contributed around 68% of overall revenue.

This indicates that geographic location can play an important role in understanding customer revenue contribution.

Transaction Method Insights

Swipe transactions generated the highest share of revenue compared with online and chip transactions.

The dashboard helped make this pattern easier to identify through visual analysis.

Customer Health Metrics

The overall activation rate was approximately 57.5%.

The overall delinquent rate was approximately 6.06%.

These metrics can help a business understand customer engagement and potential credit risk.
